@Briefest= Son of Terah
@Brief= Patriarch, chosen by God to father His people
@Short= Abraham, originally named Abram, was the patriarch whom God called to leave his homeland and blessed to become the father of many nations, establishing His covenant with him and his descendants.
@Article= Abraham, initially named Abram, was a central figure in the early history of the Israelites. He was born in Ur of the Chaldeans and was the son of Terah (<ref="Gen.11.26-31">Gen.11.26-31</ref>). God called Abram to leave his country and family and go to a land that He would show him, promising to make him a great nation and to bless all peoples through him (<ref="Gen.12.1-3">Gen.12.1-3</ref>). <BR>Abram obeyed God and journeyed to Canaan with his wife Sarai and his nephew Lot (<ref="Gen.12.4-5">Gen.12.4-5</ref>). God reaffirmed His covenant with Abram, promising him numerous descendants and the land of Canaan (<ref="Gen.13.14-17; 15.1-21">Gen.13.14-17; 15.1-21</ref>). God changed Abram's name to Abraham, meaning "father of many," and Sarai's name to Sarah (<ref="Gen.17.1-16">Gen.17.1-16</ref>). <BR>Despite their advanced age, Abraham and Sarah miraculously bore a son, Isaac, through whom God's covenant would be established (<ref="Gen.18.1-15; 21.1-7">Gen.18.1-15; 21.1-7</ref>). Abraham demonstrated his faith and obedience when God tested him by asking him to sacrifice Isaac (<ref="Gen.22.1-19">Gen.22.1-19</ref>). God provided a ram as a substitute, reaffirming His promise to Abraham (<ref="Gen.22.15-18">Gen.22.15-18</ref>). <BR>Abraham's faith was credited to him as righteousness (<ref="Gen.15.6">Gen.15.6</ref>; <ref="Rom.4.3">Rom.4.3</ref>; <ref="Gal.3.6">Gal.3.6</ref>; Jam <ref="2.23">2.23</ref>). He became known as the father of the faithful and the friend of God (<ref="Isa.41.8">Isa.41.8</ref>; <ref="2Ch.20.7">2Ch.20.7</ref>; Jam <ref="2.23">2.23</ref>). God's covenant with Abraham, which included the promise of land, descendants, and blessing, was passed down to his son Isaac and grandson Israel (or 'Jacob') (<ref="Gen.26.1-5; 28.10-15">Gen.26.1-5; 28.10-15</ref>). <BR>The life and faith of Abraham are significant in both the Old and New Testaments. He is recognized as the ancestor of the Israelites (<ref="Exo.2.24; 3.6, 15-16; 4.5">Exo.2.24; 3.6, 15-16; 4.5</ref>) and is mentioned in connection with God's faithfulness and His promises (<ref="Exo.6.8; 32.13; 33.1">Exo.6.8; 32.13; 33.1</ref>; <ref="Lev.26.42">Lev.26.42</ref>). In the New Testament, Abraham is upheld as an example of faith (<ref="Rom.4.1-3, 9, 12, 16">Rom.4.1-3, 9, 12, 16</ref>; <ref="Gal.3.6-9, 14, 29">Gal.3.6-9, 14, 29</ref>; <ref="Heb.11.8-19">Heb.11.8-19</ref>) and is identified as the spiritual father of all believers (<ref="Rom.4.11-12, 16-17">Rom.4.11-12, 16-17</ref>; <ref="Gal.3.7, 29">Gal.3.7, 29</ref>). <BR>Jesus Christ is identified as the ultimate fulfillment of God's promises to Abraham (<ref="Mat.1.1">Mat.1.1</ref>; <ref="Gal.3.16">Gal.3.16</ref>). Through faith in Christ, believers from all nations become part of Abraham's spiritual lineage and heirs to the promises made to him (<ref="Gal.3.7-9, 14, 26-29">Gal.3.7-9, 14, 26-29</ref>).
